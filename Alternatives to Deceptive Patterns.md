# Alternatives to Deceptive Patterns

Use this map to find a design alternative and test whether people can make and carry out their own choices. Start with the decision or task at risk. Follow the relevant journey guide, then use the component guidance to check the controls involved.

The map follows the 11 areas named in the [Double-FW catalogue brief](https://github.com/Double-FW/Deceptive_Patterns_and-Inclusive_Experience_Evaluation/blob/main/Deceptive_Patterns_Repository_Development_Prompt.md#5-build-the-pattern-and-practice-catalogue). Use the observed journey and its effect to assess a finding. A pattern name alone establishes neither intent nor a standards failure.

| Risk | Alternative and check | Cosmos guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concealed costs or conditions | Show the total, significant conditions and recurring charges before commitment. Check that all charges are explained and the confirmation matches. | [Informed Choice and Preferences](Informed%20Choice%20and%20Preferences.md#show-what-the-choice-means) |
| Misleading hierarchy or wording | Label each choice by its consequence. Check that each option can be found and understood visually and with assistive technology. | [Informed Choice and Preferences](Informed%20Choice%20and%20Preferences.md#make-choices-comparable), [Call to Action](Call%20to%20Action.md) |
| Obstructed refusal or cancellation | Provide a direct route to decline or cancel and confirm the outcome. | [Refusal, Cancellation and Exit](Refusal%2C%20Cancellation%20and%20Exit.md) |
| Unequal effort between choices | Compare the complete routes. Check for avoidable steps, sign-ins, waits and channel changes. | [Compare the effort](Refusal%2C%20Cancellation%20and%20Exit.md#compare-the-effort) |
| Forced disclosure or registration | Ask for information needed for the task. Offer a route without an account where the task can be delivered that way. | [Ask only for what the task needs](Informed%20Choice%20and%20Preferences.md#ask-only-for-what-the-task-needs) |
| Repeated pressure | Honour a refusal. Check that returning later does not trigger the same invitation again while that decision still applies. | [Respect a refusal](Refusal%2C%20Cancellation%20and%20Exit.md#respect-a-refusal), [Notifications and Status Messages](Notifications%20and%20Status%20Messages.md) |
| False urgency or scarcity | State the real deadline or availability. Check that the message and countdown, if present, agree with the service’s information across visits. | [Show what the choice means](Informed%20Choice%20and%20Preferences.md#show-what-the-choice-means), [Call to Action](Call%20to%20Action.md) |
| Unwanted defaults | Keep optional extras off initially and make the starting state clear. Check that a saved preference stays in effect. | [Defaults and preferences](Informed%20Choice%20and%20Preferences.md#defaults-and-preferences) |
| Consent interference | Make acceptance, refusal and later changes easy to find and operate. Verify that the service applies the same choice it shows. | [Defaults and preferences](Informed%20Choice%20and%20Preferences.md#defaults-and-preferences) |
| Inaccessible support | Offer a usable way to get help and escalate. Test a route that does not require the input or channel the person cannot use. | [Support, Escalation and Handoffs](Support%2C%20Escalation%20and%20Handoffs.md#make-support-findable-and-usable) |
| Abandonment across handoffs | Name who owns the next step and provide a recovery route. Test what happens when the receiving team or system cannot pick up the request. | [Carry the task across a handoff](Support%2C%20Escalation%20and%20Handoffs.md#carry-the-task-across-a-handoff) |

Record the route, starting state, choices offered, steps taken and outcome. Include any difference in effort or access and the evidence needed to distinguish necessary safeguards from avoidable barriers.
