# Informed Choice and Preferences

Give people the information and controls they need before they commit, then make it easy to review or change their choice. A well-labelled button can still lead to a poor decision if the cost appears later or an optional extra is selected by default.

## Show what the choice means

Put the total charge, any recurring amount and interval, significant conditions and what happens next near the decision. If a total depends on an outstanding choice, explain what is included and update the total before the person commits. Make genuine deadlines and availability specific and keep them consistent with the service’s information. Let people review and amend their choices before a consequential submission. See [Call to Action](Call%20to%20Action.md) and [Forms](Forms.md).

If a material detail changes after the person has reviewed it, use [Showing What Changed Before Confirmation](Showing%20What%20Changed%20Before%20Confirmation.md) to present the new details and obtain a fresh decision.

## Make choices comparable

Use direct labels that describe what happens, such as ‘Accept optional cookies’ and ‘Decline optional cookies’. Keep alternatives visible and reachable at the decision point. Check that styling, reading order and focus order let people discover the available routes. See [Labels](Labels.md) and [Keyboard Navigation](Keyboard%20Navigation.md).

## Ask only for what the task needs

Ask for required information when it is needed and explain its purpose if it is unclear. Offer a route without an account where the task can be delivered that way. For example, let someone buy a one-off ticket as a guest and offer an account separately. Explain any actual account requirement before the person starts.

## Defaults and preferences

Keep optional purchases and permissions unselected so the person can choose them explicitly. Identify the current state in text and expose it through the control. Make it clear which preferences are optional and what accepting or declining changes. Offer a visible settings route for checking or changing them later. Confirm changes and ensure the settings screen and actual behaviour agree. See [Notifications and Status Messages](Notifications%20and%20Status%20Messages.md#persistent-inline-feedback).

## Worked example

A service sells a ticket and offers an optional flexible booking add-on. The initial total excludes the add-on. If the person selects it, show the new total and what flexibility it provides before they submit. The checkout displays the same total at confirmation. If the add-on can be changed after purchase, explain where and under what conditions.

## Checkpoints for the tester

| Method and action | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Start from the offer and continue to the final decision. Review the total, options and conditions with keyboard and screen reader navigation. | The person can tell what the decision costs or permits. Any change is explained before commitment. |
| Start a fresh session, decline optional choices, then return and edit preferences. | Optional extras start unselected. The chosen state stays in effect and can be checked and changed later. |
| Try the guest and signed-in routes where both are available. Inspect the required fields. | Each route asks for the information it needs. Any account requirement is explained before the task begins. |
